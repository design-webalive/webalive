4  Statement — why I should be a finalist 188 words

I came to patisserie late. I was nineteen, working front of house in Footscray, and the pastry chef let me come in at five to watch her laminate. That hour became the reason I enrolled.

Two years into my Certificate IV I run the dessert section at our training restaurant three services a week. I have learned that precision is not the same as rigidity: my tutor made me remake a bavarois eleven times until I could feel when the gelatine had taken rather than reading it off a thermometer. That is the discipline I want to take further.

Thierry Marx works at the point where technique stops being decoration and starts being an idea. Ten days in that kitchen would show me how a brigade of that standard makes decisions under pressure — how they hold a line at the fortieth cover the same way they held it at the first. I would bring it back to Holmesglen, to the apprentices coming through behind me, and to a career I intend to spend in this country.

5  Recipes

Main course — Sher Wagyu striploin

Wagyu striploin, burnt onion dashi, salted koji butter

Serves 4 · 55 minutes · Original work

Ingredients
  • 600g Sher Wagyu striploin, trimmed, at room temperature
  • 3 brown onions, halved skin on
  • 400ml kombu and bonito dashi
  • 80g cultured butter, 20g shiro koji
  • Salt bush, black garlic, young leek hearts
Method
  1. Char the onions cut side down on a dry plancha until fully black, then steep in the hot dashi for 20 minutes and pass.
  2. Blend the koji through the softened butter with a pinch of salt; hold at room temperature.
  3. Seal the striploin hard in a heavy pan, basting with the koji butter, to 52°C at the centre. Rest 12 minutes.
  4. Grill the leek hearts, dress with black garlic and salt bush. Slice the beef across the grain, spoon the onion dashi at the table.
Dessert

Riverina rice pudding, quandong, brown butter tuile

Serves 4 · 2 hours · Original work

Ingredients
  • 120g Riverina medium grain rice
  • 500ml full cream milk, 200ml cream, 1 vanilla bean
  • 180g dried quandong, 90g raw sugar, juice of one lemon
  • 60g butter browned to noisette, 40g flour, 40g icing sugar
  • Bronze fennel flowers to finish
Method
  1. Cook the rice slowly in the milk, cream and vanilla, stirring, until the grain is just yielding and the liquid nappe.
  2. Poach the quandong in the sugar and lemon with 150ml water until glossy; reserve a third of the syrup.
  3. Whisk the noisette butter into the flour and icing sugar, rest, then bake thin at 175°C for 7 minutes and shape warm.
  4. Plate the pudding warm, quandong over, syrup around, tuile on the angle, fennel flowers last.
